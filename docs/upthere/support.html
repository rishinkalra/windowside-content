Tonight says there's nothing bright tonight

The space stations go through quiet spells. You can only see a station when your sky is dark but the station is still in sunlight, so passes come in runs around dusk and dawn, with gaps of a week or more between. Up There shows the next good pass as soon as there is one, and you can turn on an alert for it.

How do I see a pass?

Go outside a few minutes early and face the direction the app gives. A station looks like a bright, steady star moving smoothly across the sky, with no blinking lights. It takes a few minutes to cross. If the app says it "fades", it slips into Earth's shadow before reaching the horizon and simply disappears.

I'm not getting alerts

Allow notifications for Up There in your phone's Settings, and check that a Focus or Do Not Disturb isn't silencing them. Choose how early you're warned (5, 10 or 20 minutes for passes; 24 hours, 1 hour or 10 minutes for launches) in the app's settings.

A launch time changed

Launch times move often. Up There checks for changes when you open it and now and then in the background, and moves your alerts with the launch. Open the app on launch day to be sure you have the latest time.

Can I see a launch from where I live?

Open the launch and look for "Can I see it from here?". Up There flies a typical climb to orbit from the launch pad and works out whether it clears your horizon, which way to look, and when. Rockets can be seen hundreds of kilometres away, especially just after sunset or before sunrise.

Changing your location

Open settings (the gear at the top of Home) and choose "Run the welcome again" to use your current location or pick a different city.
