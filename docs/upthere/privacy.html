Up There Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

Up There tells you what's happening in the sky above you: when you can see the space stations pass over, which rocket launches are coming up, and how each Starship flight went. This policy explains what the app does with your information. In short: there's no account, no ads and no tracking, and your exact location stays on your phone.

Your location

Passes and launch sightings depend on where you are, so Up There uses your location, or a city you pick instead. The maths that works out passes, sky paths and whether you can see a launch runs on your phone. Your location is stored only on your phone.

Two things need help from outside the phone. To read the cloud forecast for your passes, the app sends your position rounded to about a kilometre to Open-Meteo, a free weather service, which returns the hourly cloud cover. To show the name of your town, and to find a city you type, the app uses Apple's own place lookup, under Apple's privacy policy. Neither request includes anything else about you.

What the app downloads

Up There downloads public space data: satellite orbits from CelesTrak, launches from Launch Library 2 (The Space Devs), and news from the Spaceflight News API. These requests carry no information about you. Like any website, these services can see your device's IP address when it connects, under their own privacy policies.

Alerts, Lock Screen and Apple Watch

Pass and launch alerts are scheduled on your phone; nothing is sent to a server to deliver them. Lock Screen countdowns run on your phone. The Apple Watch app gets its data straight from your iPhone.

No account, no ads, no tracking

Up There has no sign-in, shows no ads, and contains no analytics or tracking software. We don't sell or share personal information.

Bug reports in test versions

If you're a tester using a test version of Up There and you choose to send a bug report, the report contains what you add (a screenshot or a short screen recording of the app, and your note, typed or dictated) plus technical details to help fix the problem: the app version, your device model and iOS version, recent app log lines, the screen you were on, your place name and your position rounded to about 10 km. Dictation is turned into text on your phone; your voice isn't recorded or sent. Reports are sent over an encrypted connection to a server we run, used only to fix bugs, and not shared with anyone. The App Store version of the app has no bug-report feature and sends nothing of this kind.

Deleting your data

To have bug reports you sent from a test version deleted, email rishin.kalra@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my Up There reports" and roughly when you sent them. We'll delete all of them and reply to confirm. Up There keeps no other data about you: your location, settings and alerts live only on your phone and are removed when you delete the app.

Children

Up There is not directed at children under 13.
